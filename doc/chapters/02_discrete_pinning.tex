\section{Discrete requirements and the pinned force}
\label{sec:pinning}

\paragraph{Setting.} A finite-volume grid with cells $c$ of volume $V_c$ and faces $f$; $\bm v_c$ the cell
velocity, $\bm K_c$ the unit zonal direction at the cell centre, $\psi_c=\Omega s_c^2$ the planetary AAM per
unit mass at the centre. $G$ is a linear map from cell velocities to face mass fluxes,
$(G\bm v)_f=\sum_c g_{fc}\cdot\bm v_c$ (mass flux through $f$ in the direction of the face normal $\bm n_f$,
per unit density; the density is uniform in everything below and drops out), and $(d\psi)_f=\psi_{R(f)}-\psi_{L(f)}$
the jump of $\psi$ across $f$ in that direction. $G$ is \emph{compact} when $g_{fc}\ne0$ only for cells within a
fixed number of layers of $f$, and \emph{linear-exact} when $G$ applied to the cell values of a linear function of
position returns the exact face value. $C$ is the discrete Coriolis operator, $(C\bm v)_c$ the force per unit mass
on cell $c$; the inner product is $\langle\bm u,\bm w\rangle_V=\sum_c V_c\,\bm u_c\cdot\bm w_c$. Solid-body
rotation about the axis is $\bm v=\bm K$ with $|\bm K_c|=s_c$ absorbed into $\bm K_c$ in this section, i.e.\
$\bm K_c=s_c\hat{\bm e}_{\lambda,c}$.

\paragraph{Requirements.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Zero work for every state: $\langle\bm v,C\bm v\rangle_V=0$ for all $\bm v$.
\item[(b)] Exact closure of the AAM budget against the mass flux $G$: for all $\bm v$,
\[ \sum_c V_c\,\bm K_c\cdot(C\bm v)_c=-\Omega\sum_f (G\bm v)_f\,(d\psi)_f .\]
The right side is the planetary AAM carried through the faces by the mass flux $G\bm v$; (b) says the Coriolis
torque on the relative AAM equals minus that transport, which is the discrete form of $D(s u)/Dt+D\psi/Dt$
having no Coriolis part (chapter~\ref{sec:continuous}).
\item[(c)] Consistency: $C\bm K$ is the cell-form Coriolis force on solid-body rotation, $(C_{\rm cell}\bm K)_c=
-2\bm\Omega\times\bm K_c$ up to the metric term, i.e.\ $C\bm K=\nabla\psi$ at the cell centres (per unit mass,
$-2\bm\Omega\times\bm K_c-\bm K_c\cdot\nabla\bm K_c=\nabla\psi_c$ on the sphere).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{lemma}[pinning]\label{lem:pin}\derived\
Let $C$ be linear and satisfy (a) and (b). Then
\[ C\bm K=\Omega\,V^{-1}G^{T}d\psi ,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
   (C\bm K)_c=\frac{\Omega}{V_c}\sum_f g_{fc}\,(d\psi)_f , \]
where $g_{fc}$ is read as the vector weight of cell $c$ in the flux through $f$. The force on solid-body
rotation is fixed by the grid, $G$ and $\psi$ alone; $C$ has no freedom left on that state.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) for the states $\bm v$, $\bm K$ and $\bm v+\bm K$ gives, by linearity of $C$,
$\langle\bm v,C\bm K\rangle_V+\langle\bm K,C\bm v\rangle_V=0$, so $\langle\bm v,C\bm K\rangle_V=
-\langle\bm K,C\bm v\rangle_V$ for every $\bm v$: a zero-work operator is skew in the $V$-inner product.
(b) is a statement about $\langle\bm K,C\bm v\rangle_V$, so
\[ \langle\bm v,C\bm K\rangle_V=\Omega\sum_f (G\bm v)_f(d\psi)_f=\Omega\sum_f\sum_c (g_{fc}\cdot\bm v_c)(d\psi)_f
   =\Omega\sum_c \bm v_c\cdot\Big(\sum_f g_{fc}(d\psi)_f\Big)=\langle\bm v,\Omega V^{-1}G^T d\psi\rangle_V .\]
This holds for every $\bm v$, and the $V$-inner product is non-degenerate, so the two vectors agree cell by cell.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The defect.} Define $\bm e=\Omega V^{-1}G^T d\psi-\nabla\psi$ (cell by cell, $\nabla\psi$ the exact
gradient at the cell centre). By the lemma, (c) can hold only if $\bm e=0$. $\bm e$ is a property of the grid
and $G$ alone; chapter~\ref{sec:seam} measures it.

\begin{theorem}[no consistent zero-work exact closure on the cubed sphere]\label{thm:imp}\derived\
Assume (i) $C$ linear, (ii) (a) exactly, (iii) (b) exactly against a compact linear-exact $G$ built from the
panel-local stencils of a gnomonic cubed-sphere grid (two-point central, or the six-point cp5-central of
chapter~\ref{sec:seam}, with the panel-edge ghosts filled by any interpolation along the neighbour panel's edge
that reproduces linear functions). Then (c) fails: $\bm e\ne0$ in the cells adjacent to a panel seam, and
$\max_{\text{row }0}|\bm e|/(\Omega a)$ does not go to zero as the resolution increases.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof (the structure; the numbers are chapter~\ref{sec:seam})]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:pin}, (i)--(iii) pin $C\bm K$ to $\Omega V^{-1}G^T d\psi$, so (c) is equivalent to
$\bm e=0$. Away from the seams, $G^T d\psi$ is the adjoint of a panel-local difference operator applied to the
cell values of $\psi$; for a linear-exact $G$ it is $V_c\nabla\psi_c$ plus the quadratic error of the stencil,
$O(h^2)$ since $\psi$ is a quadratic polynomial of position (interior rows converge at second order in
chapter~\ref{sec:seam}). In a seam cell the stencil reaches across the seam into the neighbour panel, whose
cell centres do not continue this panel's grid lines: relative to the continuation of the own line, the
neighbour centre is offset along the seam by $\tfrac12\sin2\beta$ cells ($\beta$ the position along the edge,
chapter~\ref{sec:seam}). A linear-exact interpolation along the neighbour's edge reproduces the linear part of
$\psi$ exactly, so the error of $(d\psi)_f$ at a seam face is the quadratic part of $\psi$ evaluated at the
offset point, which is $O(h^2)$ in $\psi$ but enters $\bm e$ divided by the face spacing $h$: the kink of the
coordinate lines at the seam leaves an $O(h)\cdot O(1)/h=O(1)$ residual in the row-0 cells. The residual is
proportional to $\sin2\beta$ and vanishes only at mid-edge. Hence $\bm e\ne0$ on the seam rows at every
resolution, which is what is measured (row-0 max $0.27$--$0.33$ for $N=8$--$64$, two-point and six-point alike,
linear or cubic ghosts).
\end{proof}

What the theorem does not say: it is a statement about the combination of (a), (b) and a panel-local
compact $G$. Dropping any one escapes it. A $C$ that does work (drop (a)), a closure against a different
flux (drop (b)), or a nonlinear $C$ (drop (i)) are not covered; nor is a $G$ whose seam stencil is not
panel-local (next paragraph). The 22\,\% seam-row figure of the side study is the same quantity in a
different norm (fraction of $\max|f\rho u|$ instead of $\max|\bm e|/(\Omega a)$).

\paragraph{What an adjoint-consistent $G$ would have to satisfy.} \derived\ $\bm e=0$ reads
$G^T d\psi=\Omega^{-1}V\nabla\psi$: the adjoint of the flux operator, applied to the face jumps of
$\psi=\Omega s^2$, must return the exact cell gradient of $\psi$ times the cell volume, in every cell including
the seam rows. Since $\psi$ is a quadratic polynomial of the position vector ($\psi=\Omega(1-z^2)$ on the unit
sphere with a polar axis, and a rotated quadratic for a tilted axis), a sufficient condition is that
$G^T d$ be exact on quadratics: $G^T d\,q=V\nabla q$ for every quadratic $q$ of position. With the discrete
divergence $D=-G^T$ this is the discrete gradient theorem $\sum_c V_c\nabla q_c=-\sum_f (G^T dq)_c$ holding
cell by cell, i.e.\ $G$ and the cell gradient must be adjoint on quadratics. On a lat--lon grid the
two-point flux has this property for $\psi$ because $\psi$ is a function of latitude alone and the faces are
aligned with it; $\psi$ is then an exact invariant of discrete solid-body advection, $(G\bm K)_f(d\psi)_f=0$
face by face, and the right side of (b) vanishes on $\bm K$ as the left side does by (a). On the cubed sphere
the seam faces are not aligned with $\psi$'s level lines, $(G\bm K)_f(d\psi)_f\ne0$ there, and (b) on $\bm K$
becomes $\sum_f (G\bm K)_f(d\psi)_f=0$ as a sum, which a compact panel-local stencil does not deliver. A $G$
that satisfies the quadratic adjoint condition must use, at a seam face, a stencil that knows the neighbour
panel's geometry beyond linear interpolation along its edge: either a non-compact (edge-global) weighting that
reproduces quadratics across the kink, or a flux defined on the mirror points of chapter~\ref{sec:seam}
(the neighbour's centre is the exact mirror of the own centre through the seam great circle) with the
quadratic correction $\tfrac12\sin2\beta\,h$ applied explicitly. Whether such a $G$ exists with a stencil of
fixed width is open (chapter~\ref{sec:open}, item 1); the first-moment result below says that no compact
linear-exact re-weighting gets there.

resolution, roughly like $h^2$, for every stencil and axis. So the pinned force fails (c) by a seam-local couple of
$O(1)$ amplitude that carries no net force and no net first moment at leading order; it cannot be removed by a
compact re-weighting (below), but it also injects no net momentum or AAM into the pair of seam rows beyond $O(h^2)$,
which is why a global closure can hide it in a budget while the local field stays wrong.

\begin{claim}[moments of the defect are fixed by the face geometry]\derived\
Let $G$ have, for each face $f$, weights $g_{fc}$ that sum to the face measure, $\sum_c g_{fc}=\bm n_f l_f$, and
let $G$ be linear-exact, so that the weighted centroid of its stencil is the face midpoint,
$\sum_c g_{fc}\otimes(\bm x_c-\bm x_f)=0$. Then for any such $G$, over the whole footprint of the seam faces,
\[ \sum_c (G^T d\psi)_c=\sum_{f}(d\psi)_f\,\bm n_f l_f ,\qquad
   \sum_c (G^T d\psi)_c\otimes\bm x_c=\sum_f (d\psi)_f\,\bm n_f l_f\otimes\bm x_f , \]
both independent of the weights. A compact linear-exact re-weighting of $G$ (two-point to six-point, linear to cubic
ghosts) therefore redistributes $V\bm e$ between the rows it touches but leaves the zeroth and first moments of
$V\bm e$ over its footprint unchanged, and since those moments are the two-point values, which are $O(h^2)$ by the
table, the defect it can produce is always a couple of the same total strength. This is the mechanism behind
chapter~\ref{sec:seam}: the six-point flux moves part of the row-0 defect into row 1 (row-1 max $0.06$--$0.09$,
not converging) and lowers row 0 by a tenth, with the same moments.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
$\sum_c (G^T d\psi)_c=\sum_f (d\psi)_f\sum_c g_{fc}$ and $\sum_c(G^T d\psi)_c\otimes\bm x_c=\sum_f(d\psi)_f
\sum_c g_{fc}\otimes\bm x_c=\sum_f (d\psi)_f\,\bm n_f l_f\otimes\bm x_f$ by the two assumptions; neither depends on
how the weights are distributed among the cells. $V\nabla\psi$ does not depend on $G$ at all.
\end{proof}

The side study's statement that the first moment of $\bm e$ in rows 0, 1, 2 is $-\tfrac14,-\tfrac18,-\tfrac1{12}\,
\sin2\beta$ is \emph{not reproduced} here: the across-seam row-0 amplitude is indeed about $1/4$ at the corners,
but its profile along the edge is even in $u$, not $\sin2\beta$, and the row-1 and row-2 amplitudes of the six-point
flux are $0.06$--$0.09$ and $0.01$--$0.03$ (chapter~\ref{sec:seam}), not $1/8$ and $1/12$; for the two-point flux
rows 1 and 2 converge. Part 2 does not state the definition of its first moment, so the comparison is with the
quantities defined above; the geometric $\tfrac12\sin2\beta$ offset of chapter~\ref{sec:seam} is the only
$\sin2\beta$ in the problem that is reproduced. \hyp\ Their three coefficients may be the row-0, row-1, row-2 shares of
a $\sin2\beta$-weighted across-seam moment of a three-layer stencil; with that definition supplied they can be checked
with the script in one line.
