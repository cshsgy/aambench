\section{Discrete requirements and the pinned force}
\label{sec:pinning}

\paragraph{Setting.} A finite-volume grid with cells $c$ of volume $V_c>0$ and faces $f$; $\bm v_c\in\mathbb R^3$ the
cell velocity (every cell vector in this chapter is a vector of $\mathbb R^3$ attached to the cell centre, not a
tangential vector; the lemma therefore pins all three components of $C\bm K$), $\bm K_c$ the solid-body velocity
$\hat{\bm z}\times\bm x_c$ (so $|\bm K_c|=s_c$), $\psi_c=\Omega s_c^2$ the planetary AAM per unit mass at the centre.
$G$ is a linear map from cell velocities to face mass fluxes, $(G\bm v)_f=\sum_c \bm g_{fc}\cdot\bm v_c$ (mass flux
through $f$ along the face normal $\bm n_f$, per unit density; the density is uniform below and drops out), and
$(d\psi)_f=\psi_{R(f)}-\psi_{L(f)}$ the jump of $\psi$ across $f$ in that direction. $G$ is \emph{compact} when
$\bm g_{fc}\ne0$ only for cells within a fixed number of layers of $f$. $C$ is the discrete Coriolis operator,
$(C\bm v)_c$ the force per unit mass on cell $c$; the inner product is $\langle\bm u,\bm w\rangle_V=\sum_c V_c\,
\bm u_c\cdot\bm w_c$.

\paragraph{Requirements.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Zero work for every state: $\langle\bm v,C\bm v\rangle_V=0$ for all $\bm v$.
\item[(b)] Exact closure of the AAM budget against the mass flux $G$: for all $\bm v$,
\[ \sum_c V_c\,\bm K_c\cdot(C\bm v)_c=-\Omega\sum_f (G\bm v)_f\,(d\psi)_f .\]
The right side is the planetary AAM carried through the faces by the mass flux $G\bm v$; (b) says the Coriolis
torque on the relative AAM equals minus that transport, the discrete form of $DL/Dt$ having no Coriolis part
(chapter~\ref{sec:continuous}).
\item[(c)] Consistency: $C\bm K$ is the cell-form Coriolis force on solid-body rotation, which on the sphere is
$C\bm K=\nabla\psi$ at the cell centres ($-2\bm\Omega\times\bm K_c$ with the metric term).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{lemma}[pinning]\label{lem:pin}\derived\
Let $C$ be linear and satisfy (a) and (b). Then
\[ C\bm K=\Omega\,V^{-1}G^{T}d\psi ,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
   (C\bm K)_c=\frac{\Omega}{V_c}\sum_f \bm g_{fc}\,(d\psi)_f . \]
The force on solid-body rotation is fixed by the grid, $G$ and $\psi$ alone; $C$ has no freedom left on that state.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) for the states $\bm v$, $\bm K$ and $\bm v+\bm K$ gives, by linearity of $C$,
$\langle\bm v,C\bm K\rangle_V+\langle\bm K,C\bm v\rangle_V=0$: a zero-work operator is skew in the $V$-inner
product. (b) is a statement about $\langle\bm K,C\bm v\rangle_V$, so
\[ \langle\bm v,C\bm K\rangle_V=\Omega\sum_f (G\bm v)_f(d\psi)_f=\Omega\sum_f\sum_c (\bm g_{fc}\cdot\bm v_c)(d\psi)_f
   =\Omega\sum_c \bm v_c\cdot\Big(\sum_f \bm g_{fc}(d\psi)_f\Big)=\langle\bm v,\Omega V^{-1}G^T d\psi\rangle_V .\]
This holds for every $\bm v\in(\mathbb R^3)^{\rm cells}$, and the $V$-inner product is non-degenerate ($V_c>0$),
so the two vectors agree cell by cell and component by component. Only the linearity of $C$, (a) for all $\bm v$
and (b) for all $\bm v$ are used.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The defect.} Define $\bm e=\Omega V^{-1}G^T d\psi-\nabla\psi$ (cell by cell, $\nabla\psi$ the exact
gradient at the cell centre). By the lemma, (c) can hold only if $\bm e=0$. $\bm e$ is a property of the grid
and $G$ alone; chapter~\ref{sec:seam} and the table below measure it.

\begin{theorem}[no consistent zero-work exact closure with the panel-local fluxes]\label{thm:imp}
Assume (i) $C$ linear, (ii) (a) exactly, (iii) (b) exactly against the two-point central $G$ or the six-point
cp5-central $G$ of chapter~\ref{sec:seam}, built panel by panel on the gnomonic cubed-sphere grid with the
panel-edge ghosts filled by linear or cubic interpolation along the neighbour panel's edge (the two ghost orders
tested). Then (c) fails: $\bm e\ne0$ in the cells adjacent to a panel seam, and $\max_{\text{row }0}|\bm e|$ does
not go to zero as the resolution increases.
\end{theorem}
\noindent Status: \derived\ for the reduction (i)--(iii) $\Rightarrow$ ((c) $\Leftrightarrow\bm e=0$), by
Lemma~\ref{lem:pin}; \measured\ for $\bm e\ne0$ on row 0 and its non-convergence (chapter~\ref{sec:seam} and the
table below: row-0 max $0.19$--$0.32$ for $N=8$--$64$, both fluxes, both ghost orders, both axes, in units of
$\Omega a$). The mechanism is the next paragraph; it is derived in its structure and measured in its size.

\paragraph{Mechanism: the seam stencils are not linear-exact.} \derived\ Write $\psi$ near cell $c$ as
$\psi_c+\nabla\psi_c\cdot(\bm x-\bm x_c)+q(\bm x)$ with $q$ the quadratic remainder. Since $G^Td$ is linear in $\psi$,
\[ \bm e_c=E_c\,\nabla\psi_c+\bm r_c ,\qquad E_c=\big(V^{-1}G^T d-\nabla\big)\text{ on the linear functions }
   \bm a\cdot\bm x\ (\text{a }3\times3\text{ matrix per cell}),\qquad
   \bm r_c=\big(V^{-1}G^T d\,q\big)_c-\nabla q_c , \]
and $\bm r_c=O(h)$ for every stencil whose weights are bounded: $dq$ across a link of length $O(h)$ is $O(h^2)$, the
face measure over the cell volume is $O(1/h)$. So the only way $\bm e$ can stay $O(1)$ is $E_c=O(1)$: the stencil
must fail to reproduce the gradient of linear functions at that cell. \measured\ (last three columns of the table;
$E$ assembled with the same weights as $\bm e$, from the three functions $\bm a\cdot\bm x$): in the interior
$|E|$ falls like $h^2$ ($0.026,0.0066,0.0017,0.0004$ for the two-point flux, $N=8$--$64$), its row-0 max stays at
$0.37$--$0.42$ for every stencil, and the residual $|\bm e-E\nabla\psi|$ on row 0 falls like $h$
($0.051,0.026,0.014,0.007$ two-point polar; $0.072$--$0.012$ tilted). The orders close: the non-converging defect is
the linear-function error of the seam stencil, the quadratic part of $\psi$ contributes $O(h)$. \hyp\ Why the seam
stencil is not linear-exact (an explanation that neither the derivation nor the script tests): at a seam face the jump $(d\psi)_f$ is taken between the two mirror cells across the seam
(the neighbour's centre is the exact mirror of the own centre through the seam great circle,
chapter~\ref{sec:seam}), while the weights that deposit the jump are those of the panel-local stencil, which
reaches the neighbour panel at the continuation of the own grid line and is interpolated along the neighbour's
edge; the link and the deposit point differ by the $\tfrac12\sin2\beta$-cell offset of chapter~\ref{sec:seam}
and by the kink of the coordinate lines, an $O(h)$ mismatch divided by $h$. Linear interpolation along the
neighbour's edge (linear or cubic in the index) reproduces linear functions along that edge, but that is not the
property that the gradient needs; the mismatch is between where the jump is measured and where it is deposited.
The six-point flux spreads the same mismatch over two rows (chapter~\ref{sec:seam}), which lowers the row-0 max by
$9$--$17\,\%$ on the polar axis ($0.252\to0.209$ at $N=64$) and by about $9\,\%$ tilted, and raises row 1; the cubic
ghosts change the row-0 max by under a tenth for $N\ge16$ and by $+24\,\%$ at $N=8$ polar ($0.186\to0.231$). The profile that results is even along the edge
on the polar axis (next paragraph) because both the mirror offset and the deposit weights are symmetric under
$\beta\to-\beta$ there.

What the theorem does not say: it is a statement about (a), (b) and these two panel-local $G$. Dropping any one
escapes it: a $C$ that does work, a closure against a different flux, a nonlinear $C$, or a $G$ whose seam stencil
is linear-exact (next paragraph) are not covered. The side study's 22\,\% seam-row figure (its part 1,
thread post p1794706777579419; a fraction of $\max|f\rho u|$, no command or commit, not run in snapy) is the same
defect in a different norm and is quoted, not reproduced.

\paragraph{What $G$ would have to satisfy for $\bm e=0$.} \derived\ The necessary and sufficient condition for the
given axis is $G^T d\psi=\Omega^{-1}V\nabla\psi$ for that one $\psi$. If $\bm e=0$ is required for every axis, $\psi$
ranges over $\Omega(a^2-(\hat{\bm\Omega}\cdot\bm x)^2)$, so the condition becomes exactness of $V^{-1}G^T d$ on the
homogeneous quadratic forms $\bm x^T A\bm x$ (the span of $(\bm a\cdot\bm x)^2$); it does not ask for exactness on
linear functions, and exactness on all quadratics of position is sufficient but more than needed. With the
discrete divergence $D=-G^T$ this is the discrete gradient theorem holding cell by cell on those forms. On a
lat--lon grid the two-point flux has it for $\psi$ because $\psi$ depends on latitude alone and the faces are
aligned with its level lines; $\psi$ is then an exact invariant of discrete solid-body advection,
$(G\bm K)_f(d\psi)_f=0$ face by face, and the right side of (b) vanishes on $\bm K$ as the left side does by (a).
On the cubed sphere the seam faces are not aligned with the level lines and the panel-local stencils are not
linear-exact there (previous paragraph). A $G$ with $\bm e=0$ must therefore use, at a seam face, a stencil whose
deposit points and link agree to $O(h^2)$: a flux defined on the mirror points with the deposit interpolated at the
mirror point rather than at the grid-line continuation, or an edge-global weighting that reproduces the forms
$\bm x^TA\bm x$ across the kink. Whether such a $G$ exists with a stencil of fixed width is open
(chapter~\ref{sec:open}, item 1); nothing below settles it.

\begin{claim}[zeroth and first moments of $G^T d\psi$ over the seam-face footprint]\derived\
Let $G$ have, for each face $f$, weights whose sum is the face measure, $\sum_c\bm g_{fc}=\bm n_f l_f$. Then
$\sum_c (G^T d\psi)_c=\sum_f (d\psi)_f\,\bm n_f l_f$ over the footprint of any set of faces, whatever the weights
(exactly). If in addition the weighted deposit point of every face is its midpoint,
$\sum_c \bm g_{fc}\otimes(\bm x_c-\bm x_f)=0$, then also $\sum_c (G^T d\psi)_c\otimes\bm x_c=\sum_f (d\psi)_f\,
\bm n_f l_f\otimes\bm x_f$, independent of the weights.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
$\sum_c (G^T d\psi)_c=\sum_f (d\psi)_f\sum_c \bm g_{fc}$, and $\sum_c(G^T d\psi)_c\otimes\bm x_c=\sum_f(d\psi)_f
\sum_c \bm g_{fc}\otimes\bm x_c=\sum_f (d\psi)_f\,\bm n_f l_f\otimes\bm x_f$ under the second assumption.
\end{proof}
\measured\ (the footprint lines of the results file: the force deposited by the faces of \emph{one} seam alone, its
moments $\bm M_0=\sum_c(G^Td\psi)_c$ and $\bm M_1=\sum_c(G^Td\psi)_c\otimes\bm x_c$ compared between the three stencils
seam by seam, max over the 12 seams; the sum over all seams vanishes by symmetry and would test nothing): the zeroth
moment agrees between the two-point and the two six-point fluxes to $8\times10^{-17}$ at every $N$ and axis, against
per-seam $|\bm M_0|=0.15,0.075,0.038,0.019$ (polar, $N=8$--$64$), as the claim says. The first-moment hypothesis holds
only approximately for these stencils, because the ghosts are interpolated linearly in the index along a curved
neighbour row: the first moments differ by $7.5\times10^{-4},9.6\times10^{-5},1.2\times10^{-5},1.5\times10^{-6}$
($N=8$--$64$, polar, against per-seam $|\bm M_1|=0.098,0.050,0.025,0.013$), i.e.\ like $h^3$ (a ratio of 8 per
doubling); tilted $5.6\times10^{-4}$ to $1.1\times10^{-6}$ against $0.074$--$0.0094$. What follows, and no more:
a re-weighting of the seam faces that keeps the weight sums leaves the net $V\bm e$ of the footprint unchanged, and one
whose deposit points stay at the face midpoints leaves its first moment unchanged to $O(h^3)$. Both moments are
$O(h^2)$ already for the two-point flux, so they constrain a re-weighted defect only to an average $|\bm e|=O(h)$ over
a footprint of width $O(h)$; they do not bound its pointwise size, and they say nothing about the zero-moment part. \hyp\
Whether a compact re-weighting exists that removes the row-0 defect is therefore open; the moments do not forbid it.
The measured fact is only that the six-point flux moves part of the defect from row 0 into row 1 (row-1 max
$0.06$--$0.09$ in chapter~\ref{sec:seam}'s unit, not converging) and lowers the row-0 max by $9$--$17\,\%$.

The side study's statement that the first moment of $\bm e$ in rows 0, 1, 2 is $-\tfrac14,-\tfrac18,-\tfrac1{12}\,
\sin2\beta$ is \emph{not reproduced} here: the across-seam row-0 amplitude is $0.17$--$0.25$ at the corners in units of
$\Omega a$ ($0.22$--$0.33$ in chapter~\ref{sec:seam}'s unit, which is where the "about $1/4$" of earlier drafts came
from), its profile along the edge on the polar axis is even in $u$, not $\sin2\beta$, and the row-1 and row-2
amplitudes of the six-point flux are $0.06$--$0.09$ and $0.01$--$0.03$ (chapter~\ref{sec:seam}, in its unit), not $1/8$ and
$1/12$; for the two-point flux rows 1 and 2 converge. Part 2 does not state the definition of its first moment, so the
comparison is with the quantities defined above; the geometric $\tfrac12\sin2\beta$ offset of chapter~\ref{sec:seam}
is the only $\sin2\beta$ in the problem that is reproduced. \hyp\ Their three coefficients may be the row-0, row-1,
row-2 shares of a $\sin2\beta$-weighted across-seam moment of a three-layer stencil; with that definition supplied
(asked for in the thread) they can be checked with the script in one line.
